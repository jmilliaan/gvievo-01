Wiring and Safety Designer configuration for the two-source stop chain: emergency stop and lidar OSSD into the FX3, dual-channel STO out to both drive amplifiers, with EDM confirmation returning. Work top to bottom; tick as you go.

0 / 0 steps

Scope — read before starting

This builds the Category 0 stop chain only: a demand from either source removes torque from both drives immediately. It is the correct foundation and it is not the shippable ISO 3691-4 configuration. Still absent: the SS1 delayed-off timer driving QSTOP, safe speed monitoring from the DFS60S encoders via the FX3-MOC0, speed-dependent protective field switching, and a safety-switched brake circuit.

Two blocking findings in bldc-compliance.md (F-01 prohibited dynamic braking, F-13 single brake serving two duties) remain open and gate the protective field geometry. Nothing here closes them.

A

Wire it

Power off · 6 steps
A1

Remove all three factory jumpers — on both drivers

Out of the box each BLVD-KRD ships with 25–11, 12–26 and 13–27 linked, putting both HWTO photocouplers in series across the drive's own rail. STO is defeated and the drive behaves perfectly, which is exactly why this gets missed.

Pull all six jumpers. 25 (+V) and 13 (0V) then stay unconnected for good — the manual's host-controller diagram feeds the HWTO inputs entirely from your 24 V and 0 V.

Witness this step and record it. It is the single most common BLV-R commissioning failure and it is invisible afterwards. Finding F-09.
blvr_operatingmanual_installation.pdf p.19 note · p.23 diagram · opman_fun p.212
A2

Supply both modules and bond the grounds

A1 = +24 V, A2 = 0 V on the CPU and separately on the XTIO. The CPU's A1/A2 feeds logic and test outputs across the station via FLEXBUS+; the XTIO's own A1/A2 carries the Q1–Q4 load current and is not bussed.

Tie the nanoScan3's 0 V to the same star point as A2. Without a common reference the OSSD signal floats and the inputs read nonsense.

Limit the XTIO supply to 4 A externally — power supply or a 4 A gG fuse, characteristic B or C.
A3

Emergency stop → I1 / I2, fed from the test outputs

Both NC contacts now go to the controller instead of breaking motor current directly.

The odd/even rule is not cosmetic: it guarantees the two channels carry different pulse patterns, which is what lets the module catch a cross-circuit between them. Loop resistance must stay under 100 Ω.

A4

Lidar OSSD → I3 / I4, with no test outputs

Scanner pin 2 (OSSD 1.A) to I3, scanner pin 4 (OSSD 1.B) to I4. Nothing from X1/X2 touches these.

The nanoScan3 already pulses its own outputs at roughly 300 µs and reports a fault itself if the pulse doesn't return. Adding XTIO test pulses would put two sources on one wire. The XTIO input filter ignores pulses under 0.9 ms anyway, so the scanner's self-test passes through invisibly.

Each HWTO input draws about 3.6 mA through its 6.6 kΩ series resistor, so two in parallel is roughly 7 mA against the output's 2 A rating.

Paralleling by channel rather than dedicating Q1/Q2 to the left drive and Q3/Q4 to the right does two things: both drives receive bit-identical commands with identical timing, which is the symmetry requirement that keeps a differential tugger from yawing under braking; and it leaves two outputs free.

Two independent outputs, not one output split. One contact interrupting both channels — which is what a single NC button in the 13–27 link gives you — is a single point of failure and caps the architecture at Category 1.

No test output on these — EDM is a powered transistor output, not a dry contact. The electrical fit works without a dropper: the drive sources up to 10 mA with 2.0 V saturation, the XTIO input draws 2.4–3.8 mA, and 22 V clears the 13 V HIGH threshold comfortably.

Without EDM monitored the TÜV certificate does not apply. The PL d / SIL 2 claim on opman_fun p.210 carries an asterisk: "It is necessary to monitor the EDM output using an external device." This is the single biggest upgrade over the current bench wiring.
B

Project and hardware configuration

Flexi Soft Designer · 4 steps
Use Flexi Soft Designer for the FX3, not Safety Designer. Safety Designer handles FX3-CPU0 and FX3-XTIO perfectly well, but FX3-MOC0 is listed as N. a. — not available — in Safety Designer (Designer OI §3 Table 1). Your MOC0 and the DFS60S safe-speed chain are a planned addition, and projects do not migrate between the two tools: "Partial applications that have been created using Safety Designer and partial applications that have been created using Flexi Soft Designer are not compatible with one another." Building here in Safety Designer means rebuilding the whole station later.

So the scanner stays in Safety Designer with its own verification report, and the FX3 station lives in Flexi Soft Designer with its own. They connect over hardwired OSSDs, not over a configuration link, so they never needed to share a project — two device reports in the technical file is normal. Minimum versions for your hardware: Flexi Soft Designer V1.7.1, CPU firmware ≥ V2.50.0 for the MOC0 (yours is V4.05.0).

B1

Add the modules in the right order

New project in Flexi Soft Designer. Drag FX3-CPU0 from the Modules window into the configuration area, then add FX3-XTIO to its right — module order in the software must match the physical order on the DIN rail. Leave a slot in your thinking for the MOC0; it goes on the same rail later.

Set the Step version on the XTIO to match your hardware. Yours is FX3-XTIO84002; check the Step marking on the module label. Step 2.xx needs firmware ≥ V2.00.0, Step 3.xx needs ≥ V3.00.0. Choosing a Step the module can't run is the usual cause of a refused transfer.

B2

Connect and log in

Connect to the CPU over USB or RS-232, then Device › Detect project — it reads the physical station, loads the module layout into the hardware configuration and offers to pull the existing configuration. Useful for confirming what is actually on the rail, but note it closes whatever project is open first.

Log in as Authorized client, default password SICKSAFE. Machine operator cannot transfer; Maintenance personnel can only push already-verified configurations.

On a virgin system plug the first Authorized-client login forces a new password. It is stored in the system plug, so it survives a CPU swap. Record it in the project file — there is no recovery.
B3

Name everything before you wire the logic

Use Edit tag names in the hardware configuration. Tag names propagate into the logic editor, the report and the diagnostics view, and retro-fitting them after the logic is drawn is tedious.

Suggested: ESTOP_PANEL, LIDAR_FRONT_OSSD, EDM_DRIVE_L, EDM_DRIVE_R, STO_CH1, STO_CH2.

B4

Enter the project identification

Project name, application name, user name and a description. These land in the report header, which is the document that goes into the technical file. An unnamed report is an auditor's first question.

C

Elements — where the real work happens

5 steps

This is the part worth understanding rather than clicking through. When you drop a predefined dual-channel input element onto a pair of XTIO inputs, the module itself performs the dual-channel evaluation — equivalence, sequence and discrepancy timing all happen in hardware, before the signal reaches the logic. You then do not need an Emergency stop or Light curtain function block in the logic editor at all, and the logic stays short, which keeps the logic execution time down.

C1

Emergency stop element onto I1 / I2

From the Elements window drag the emergency-stop pushbutton element onto I1. It claims I1+I2 as a pair. Double-click it to open Element settings:

  • Evaluation: dual-channel equivalent (your button is 2 × NC, so both channels open together)
  • Test pulses: X1 on channel A, X2 on channel B
  • Discrepancy time: enabled
  • ON-OFF filter: leave off unless contact bounce causes nuisance faults
Sizing the discrepancy time. It must exceed test gap + max OFF-ON delay of the test output in use, or a normal test pulse looks like a channel discrepancy. Both figures appear in the report under Configuration › I/O module › Test pulse parameters — generate the report, read them, then set the time. Start from the element default and only raise it if you get nuisance trips; a longer time also lengthens fault detection.
C2

ESPE / OSSD element onto I3 / I4

Drag the safety-laser-scanner or OSSD-type element onto I3. Settings:

  • Evaluation: dual-channel equivalent
  • Test pulses: none — explicitly not X1 or X2
  • Discrepancy time: enabled, short. The scanner switches both OSSDs together within microseconds, so a genuine discrepancy here means a real wiring fault, not a timing artefact.

If the element list has a nanoScan3 or microScan3 entry, take it — it arrives pre-set for OSSD behaviour. A generic ESPE element configured as above is equivalent.

C3

Two single-channel elements onto I5 and I6

One per drive, for the EDM feedback. Single-channel, no test pulses. These are not a safety-sensing pair — they are two independent status signals that the logic will combine.

C4

Output elements onto Q1 and Q2 — leave test pulses ON

Drop an output element on each. In its settings you will find Enable the test pulses of this output. Leave it checked.

This resolves the open F-09 concern rather than working around it. The XTIO's output test gap is under 650 µs at a minimum 200 ms interval; the BLVD-KRD ignores HWTO dropouts shorter than 1 ms by design. No spurious power removal, and you keep full cross-circuit and short-to-24 V detection on the outputs.

Do not deactivate them. Unchecking that box degrades the safety parameters of all four outputs on the module — not just the one — and obliges you to use protected or separate cabling plus an annual simultaneous all-off test.

C5

Consider fast shut-off

Routing the E-stop through the controller adds logic and FLEXBUS+ latency that direct contact-breaking did not have. The XTIO's fast shut-off function bypasses logic processing and bus transit to drop the outputs directly, at the cost of extending input and output device response times by 8 ms.

Whether it is worth it depends on your stopping-distance budget. Prove the plain chain first, then decide with numbers.

D

Logic

4 steps

Double-click the main module in the hardware configuration to open the logic editor. Because the XTIO pre-evaluated both dual-channel pairs in Phase C, the whole program is four blocks.

D1

AND the two stop sources

Drag the ESTOP_PANEL input (the evaluated I1/I2 pair, which appears as one signal) and LIDAR_FRONT_OSSD (the I3/I4 pair) into an AND block. Output is 1 only when the button is released and the protective field is clear.

D2

AND the two EDM feedbacks

A second AND block taking EDM_DRIVE_L (I5) and EDM_DRIVE_R (I6). This single signal now means both drives confirm power removed. If either drive fails to confirm, the combined signal stays 0 and the monitoring block below will catch it.

D3

External device monitoring block → Q1, Q2

Set Max. feedback delay to 100 ms. It must exceed the logic execution time, and the drive answers within 15 ms, so 100 ms is comfortable without being slack. Enable Use Fault present.

The polarity works out with no inversion. The block expects the feedback to be the inverted value of Control. The BLVD-KRD asserts EDM only when both HWTO are OFF. So Control = 0 (outputs dropped, power removed) → EDM = 1 as expected, and Control = 1 (motor excited) → EDM = 0. The two devices agree natively.
D4

Decide the restart behaviour

As drawn, the chain re-enables the moment both sources clear. Fine for a bench rig. For the vehicle, ISO 3691-4 requires that a truck does not restart automatically after a protective stop without a deliberate condition — finding F-17.

To add it, insert a Restart block between D1 and D3: safety chain into Release 1, an acknowledge pushbutton into Restart, block output into the EDM block's Control. Minimum restart pulse 100 ms or 350 ms; the Restart required output pulses at 1 Hz and drives an indicator lamp.

The acknowledge input must be laid with protection and must not reference a test output — a short to 24 V on that line can produce a restart pulse when short-circuit detection resets the signal.
D5

Read the logic execution time

Open the FB group info tab. Every timing value you set — discrepancy times in Phase C, the 100 ms feedback delay in D3 — must exceed this number, and each carries ±10 ms plus one execution time of accuracy. With five blocks it will be small, but check rather than assume, and re-check if you add the Restart block or fast shut-off.

E

Transfer, verify, report

4 steps
E1

Validate, then transfer

Run the configuration validation in the logic editor and clear every message before transferring. Then transfer to the device — you must be logged in as Authorized client for an unverified configuration.

E2

Verify

Verify in the hardware configuration reads the configuration back from the device and compares it against the project. Confirm the result. Until you do, the device reports as unverified and the configuration has no standing in the safety file.

E3

Start the main module

The station sits in Stop after a transfer. Use Start. Nothing switches until you do — the same trap as the scanner's "device was stopped" task.

E4

Generate and archive the report

Create the report and save it as PDF. Check that it contains the test pulse parameters you needed in C1, the logic execution time, the I/O matrix and both checksums. File it alongside the nanoScan3 verification report — together they document the whole stop chain.

Before you walk away from the laptop, switch back to the Machine operator user group so an unattended session cannot transfer a configuration.
F

Commissioning tests

Witness and record · 7 steps

Each test proves one fault is actually detected, not merely that the machine stops. A chain that stops correctly but detects nothing is Category 1 wearing Category 3 clothing — and it will pass a casual demonstration.

F1

Jumpers confirmed removed, both drivers

Visual, witnessed, recorded with the driver serial numbers. Everything below is meaningless if a jumper remains.

F2

Normal stop — both channels drop

Command a stop. Expect: both drives non-excited, both EDM inputs go HIGH, the FX3 shows no fault, and the chain re-enables when the demand clears.

F3

Fault injection — Q1 open

Disconnect Q1 only. Expect: drives still de-energise (per the drive's own table, ON/OFF on the two channels gives non-excitation), but EDM stays LOW because it asserts only when both HWTO are off — so the EDM block must raise EDM error and Fault present, and the FX3 must latch.

A chain that quietly keeps working here is not Category 3.

F4

Fault injection — Q2 open

Repeat F3 on the other channel. Then repeat both with each drive individually, so you prove the left and right EDM paths independently rather than trusting the AND.

F5

E-stop, one channel at a time

Open contact 1 only, leave it open past the discrepancy time. Expect a discrepancy error — the MS LED flashing red/green at 1 Hz and the affected input LEDs flashing green — not merely a stop. Repeat for contact 2.

F6

Cross-circuit between E-stop channels

Bridge the two channels. The pulse patterns from X1 and X2 now arrive on the wrong inputs and the module must report a cross-circuit. This is the test that proves the odd/even test-output rule was applied correctly in C1.

F7

Lidar demand, end to end

Walk the protective field. Expect the same chain to run: OSSD drops, FX3 drops Q1/Q2, both drives de-energise, both EDM confirm. Time it if you can — the scanner alone contributes 130 ms before the FX3 and the drives add theirs.

Then diarise the recurring obligations: STO verification test every 3 months with results recorded, or the PL d claim lapses.

Do not

  • Deactivate the Q1–Q4 test pulses. Unnecessary here, and it degrades all four outputs on the module.
  • Reference the OSSD or EDM inputs to a test output. One is already self-tested, the other is a powered transistor output.
  • Reference the restart-acknowledge input to a test output. Short-circuit detection can synthesise a restart pulse.
  • Assign FREE to any direct input on the drivers, and block it in the fieldbus command map. It releases both holding brakes from an ordinary, non-safety-rated input — finding F-03.
  • Leave ETO reset action at level mode. Lock it to 1 — ON edge so a controller holding ETO-CLR asserted cannot auto-restart the drive — finding F-17.
  • Treat this chain as complete. It is Category 0 power removal. SS1, safe speed and field switching are still to come.
